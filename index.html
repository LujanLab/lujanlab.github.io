Department of Anatomy and Cell Biology

What we study

We study how latent neural dynamics give rise to adaptive and maladaptive behavior. We use data-driven tools to connect cells, circuits and whole-animal behavior in rodents, with a focus on how dopamine, endocannabinoids and oxytocin support motivation and learning, with a particular interest in substance use disorders.

Our research projects

Neuropixels

Q1Why do some brains walk away from drugs?

Not everyone who uses drugs loses control over their use. We record hundreds of striatal and cortical neurons at once with Neuropixels while mice self-administer drugs, look for the activity that marks a move away from seeking, and test it with closed-loop optogenetics.

Development

Q2Does the pregnant brain process rewards differently?

Pregnancy reshapes the brain about as much as adolescence does, yet we know little about what drugs taken during it do to the mother. We follow her oxytocin and dopamine signaling after birth with in vivo imaging, electrophysiology and behavioral economics.

Q3How do endocannabinoids decide which cues get our attention?

Dopamine has long been credited with tagging the cues that matter, but endocannabinoids in the prefrontal cortex also steer attention toward cues that predict reward or punishment. We take apart the molecular machinery behind this lipid signal and record how it changes the way cortical neurons respond to those cues.
